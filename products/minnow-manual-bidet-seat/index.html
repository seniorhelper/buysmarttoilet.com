The honest starting point for anyone curious about a bidet. It runs entirely on your household water pressure — no outlet, no wiring, no electrician, no permission from a landlord.

No power means no obstacle

Most people who want a bidet seat and discover their bathroom has no GFCI outlet behind the toilet stop right there. This is the version that doesn't care. If your bathroom has a toilet and a water supply, you're done.

Two nozzles, not one

Separate dedicated nozzles for feminine and rear wash rather than one wand doing both jobs. Both rinse themselves automatically before and after every use. A smooth side lever controls direction and pressure.

Built to disappear

Slim, low-profile design that doesn't leave your toilet looking like it has equipment bolted to it. Soft-close lid. Quick-release mounting so you can pop the whole seat off to clean underneath — the spot that gets ignored on every seat that doesn't do this.

What you're giving up

We'd rather be straight with you: no heated seat, no warm water, no dryer. The wash is at supply temperature. In summer that's a non-issue. In a cold climate in February, it's noticeable. If warm water is what you're after, step up to the electric Smart Bidet Seat at $299 — but you'll need that outlet.

Fit

Fits most elongated toilets, including the ANZZI two-piece TL-AZ070. Dimensions are 19.53 in. L × 14.49 in. W × 2.56 in. H. cUPC certified. Installs with basic household tools.
