Smart toilet or bidet seat? How to choose

By Zach Wennstedt · Updated November 10, 2026 · Prices checked November 10, 2026

If your current toilet flushes well, buy a bidet seat. You get warm water, a heated seat and a dryer for roughly a quarter to a third of the price of a smart toilet, and it installs in under an hour with no plumber. Buy a full smart toilet when the toilet itself needs replacing, when you're remodeling, or when you want a tankless, low-profile look and features a seat can't add, like auto flush.

The difference in one sentence

A bidet seat replaces only your toilet seat. A smart toilet replaces the whole fixture: bowl, tank (or no tank), flush mechanism and seat, built as one unit with its own electronics.

When the bidet seat is the right call

  • Your toilet is under about 15 years old, flushes cleanly and isn't cracked or rocking.
  • You rent, or you might move in the next few years.
  • You want to try a bidet before committing. Plenty of people start with a manual seat ($149.99) and only then decide whether warm water matters to them.
  • The budget is firm. The money saved can pay for a comfort-height toilet underneath the seat later.

When the full smart toilet is worth it

  • The toilet needs replacing anyway (old 3.5-gallon flush, cracked tank, constant running). Replacing it with a smart toilet means one install instead of two.
  • You're remodeling and want the clean, tankless look. A seat on a standard toilet will always look like an add-on.
  • You want features that live in the toilet, not the seat, such as automatic flushing or a foot sensor flush. Check each model's page; not every smart toilet has every feature.

Two checks before you buy either one

1. Bowl shape. Seats come in round and elongated, and the wrong one won't seal. Use our round vs elongated fit checker.

2. The outlet. Electric seats and smart toilets plug in, and a bathroom outlet should be GFCI-protected. If there's no outlet within reach of the cord, either budget for an electrician or go manual. Follow the manufacturer's manual on extension cords; brands differ, and several say not to use one at all.

A middle path a lot of people miss

A new comfort-height toilet plus an electric bidet seat. For example, the ADA comfort-height toilet ($399) with the Smart Bidet Seat 107 ($349). You get the right seat height for aging knees, a water-efficient flush and the warm-water features, and if the seat ever fails you replace a seat, not a toilet.

Questions people ask

Is a smart toilet better than a bidet seat?

Not automatically. The wash, heat and dryer features are similar. A smart toilet adds the fixture itself (flush, height, looks) and sometimes auto-flush. If your toilet is fine, a bidet seat gets you most of the benefit for much less.

Can I install a bidet seat myself?

Usually, yes. If you can unbolt a toilet seat and tighten a water fitting by hand, you can install most bidet seats. The electric ones also need an outlet nearby. If you're unsure about the water connection, a handyman can do it in under an hour.

Do smart toilets need a special drain?

No. They set on a standard floor flange like a regular toilet. Check the rough-in (the distance from the wall to the center of the drain, commonly 12 inches) before ordering.

Will a bidet seat fit my toilet?

It needs to match your bowl shape (round or elongated) and the bolt spacing. Most seats fit standard bolt spacing. Measure from the center of the seat bolts to the front edge of the bowl.
